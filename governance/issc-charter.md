> **Purpose:** This charter establishes the mandate, authority, membership, and operating rules of the Information Security Steering Committee (ISSC). It is the document that makes security governance auditable: who decides, how they meet, and what they oversee.
>
> **How to use this template:** Replace every `[___]` field. Make the membership table role-based, not name-based, so the charter survives staff changes. Have the CEO sign the APPROVAL block before the first meeting. Review annually or when the ISMS scope changes.

## 1. PURPOSE

1.1. The ISSC is the senior management body that oversees the Information Security Management System (ISMS) of [ORG NAME] ("the Organization").

1.2. The ISSC ensures that information security risk is managed in line with the Organization's risk appetite, that the security program is adequately resourced, and that progress and issues are visible to executive leadership.

## 2. AUTHORITY

2.1. The ISSC derives its authority from Section 3.3 of the Information Security Policy ([ORG]-ISP-001), which establishes the committee and its oversight role. All ISSC decisions trace to that delegation.

2.2. In the IIA Three Lines Model, the ISSC operates between the governing body and management: it provides the governance oversight delegated by the CEO, while day-to-day security operations remain with the CISO (first line) and risk/compliance functions (second line). Internal audit (third line) observes but does not vote.

2.3. The ISSC reports to the Chief Executive Officer and escalates risks, decisions, and resource requests that exceed its authority.

3.2. The Chair may invite subject-matter experts for specific agenda items; invited experts have no voting rights.

## 4. MEETING CADENCE

4.1. The ISSC meets **monthly** (minimum quarterly). Standing day/time: [______].

4.2. Extraordinary meetings are convened by the Chair within [72 hours] of a major incident, material regulatory change, or critical audit finding.

4.3. Minutes of each meeting are recorded using the ISSC Meeting Minutes template ([ORG]-GOV-002) and approved at the next meeting.

## 5. STANDARD AGENDA (THE GOVERNANCE PULSE)

5.1. Each regular meeting follows the governance pulse:

| Agenda Item | Owner | Purpose |
| :---------- | :----- | :------- |
| Risk Register review | Risk Manager | Review top risks and treatment progress |
| Incident review | CISO | Incidents since last meeting; lessons learned |
| Compliance status | Compliance Officer | Regulatory obligations, audit findings, SoA updates |
| Program updates | CISO | Projects, metrics, budget burn |
| Budget and resources | Chair | Approve spend and resource requests |

## 6. DECISION AUTHORITY

6.1. The ISSC approves: ISMS scope, risk treatment plan, major policy changes, acceptance of residual risks above [risk score] per the Risk Assessment Methodology (RAM), and the security budget.

6.2. The ISSC does not decide: individual access requests, routine operational matters, or items reserved for the Board/CEO ([list]: [______]).

## 7. QUORUM AND VOTING

7.1. Quorum is [50% + 1] of voting members, including the Chair or a designated delegate.

7.2. Decisions pass by simple majority of voting members present; the Chair holds the deciding vote in a tie.

7.3. Decisions that change risk appetite or require unbudgeted spend above [amount] are escalated to the CEO.

## 8. CHARTER AMENDMENT

8.1. Amendments may be proposed by any voting member, reviewed by the ISSC, and take effect upon CEO approval.

8.2. Every amendment is recorded in the DOCUMENT CONTROL table below.
